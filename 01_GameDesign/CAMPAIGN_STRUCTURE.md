# DROPi Tycoon Campaign Structure

## Purpose

This document converts `01_GameDesign/STORY_BIBLE.md` into a guided campaign spine that can be implemented later by mission/runtime and visual-storytelling owners without fabricating gameplay systems.

It defines:

- the opening premise;
- the first 10–20 minutes;
- the first hour;
- the first 10 hours;
- Act I–V escalation;
- long-term narrative compatibility across career paths;
- narrative dependency gates against real project systems/issues;
- scripted versus emergent beat rules;
- persistent-consequence expectations.

This is narrative architecture only. It does not define mission state machines, reward formulas, runtime data types, dialogue UI, visual assets or economy implementation.

---

# 1. Campaign Identity

Working campaign spine:

**THE ROUTES WE LEAVE BEHIND**

This title is a narrative label, not a required player-facing marketing title.

The campaign begins with one person trying to finish one useful shift and grows toward a world where that person may influence organizations, infrastructure, production and international logistics.

The campaign has no traditional hard ending.

Its purpose is to give the sandbox an emotional spine and memorable milestones while preserving freedom after each milestone.

---

# 2. Campaign Structure Model

The campaign uses four layers simultaneously.

## Layer A — Personal Need

Questions:

- Can I afford ordinary life?
- Can I finish the work without exhausting myself?
- What can I learn?
- What kind of career do I want?

## Layer B — Relationships

Questions:

- Who trusts me?
- Who depends on me?
- Who competes with me?
- Who remembers what I did?

## Layer C — Organization

Questions:

- Which employer or organization do I belong to?
- What responsibility do I accept?
- Do I remain a professional employee, become a specialist/manager, or create something new?
- Can the organization survive its own costs and promises?

## Layer D — World Consequence

Questions:

- Which needs are being satisfied?
- Which businesses are growing or failing?
- Which supply chains are working?
- Which infrastructure exists because somebody built/operated it?
- What changed in Brăila, Romania and the wider world?

The player should feel all four layers before late game. Scale changes; the basic logic does not.

# 3. Prologue — The First Day

## 3.1 Opening Situation

The player starts in or near Old Town with:

- a basic temporary place to sleep for the opening grace period;
- nearly no disposable Personal Money;
- walking transport only;
- a basic smartphone;
- basic light-delivery capability;
- a first shift at Northstar Parcel Logistics;
- no company ownership;
- no private HQ;
- no guaranteed stability beyond productive work.

The opening should communicate this through environment, phone messages and short character interaction rather than a large biography panel.

The player’s chosen name/presentation may vary. The economic situation does not.

## 3.2 First Phone Message

Ana Stoica sends a concise first-shift instruction:

- report to the Northstar work/depot context at Station Commons;
- be on time;
- bring the phone;
- expect walking/light cargo only;
- no implication that the player owns the employer or its assets.

The phone therefore begins as a useful in-world work tool, not a global admin console.

## 3.3 First Meeting

At Station Commons:

- Ana establishes standards and gives only information the character needs now;
- Radu humanizes the workplace and signals that other workers also have financial/career goals;
- the player sees that Northstar is already functioning without them;
- the first assignment is framed as a real customer commitment, not a tutorial waypoint.

## 3.4 First Delivery — “One Small Thing”

Narrative target:

A small packet of ordinary business consumables is waiting for Mirela Stan’s shop in Brăila Commerce. The exact product identity must come from the authoritative order/inventory system when implemented; a suitable example is receipt/label/packaging material.

Why it matters:

Mirela is not waiting because the tutorial needs a target. Her business needs the item to process or prepare real customer orders.

The player learns:

- pickup/custody;
- route/navigation;
- walking/Work Capacity pressure;
- delivery/handoff;
- recipient reaction;
- the idea that one small failure can propagate into somebody else’s work.

The first completion must not create arbitrary “quest money.” Compensation follows the employee/wage economy.

## 3.5 First Human Memory

Mirela reacts to how the job was performed.

She should be capable of remembering later whether the player:

- arrived reliably;
- communicated a delay where the future mission system supports it;
- handled cargo correctly;
- repeatedly disappointed her.

This is the first proof that the world remembers more than completion count.

## 3.6 First Glimpse of a Larger City

The route or follow-up beat should expose at least two different Brăila identities rather than keeping the player in one anonymous block.

Preferred opening contrast:

- Station Commons = work and movement;
- Brăila Commerce = merchant pressure;
- Old Town = household/customer intimacy.

Canal & Quays and Foundry Quarter should be visible or mentioned early as promises of larger systems without making them immediately playable requirements.

# 4. First 10–20 Minutes

These are player-experience timing targets, not World Clock durations.

## Minute 0–3 — “Report for Work”

Player understands:

- who they are economically;
- they need work;
- Northstar is employer, not owned company;
- the smartphone matters;
- Brăila is the starting city.

Character:

- Ana introduced.

System dependencies:

- World Identity;
- fresh employee state;
- basic phone/work surface;
- world movement.

## Minute 3–7 — “You’re Not the Only One Starting Small”

Player meets Radu.

The conversation establishes:

- coworkers have their own goals;
- bicycle/technical progression exists as aspiration, not automatic unlock;
- work has shifts/capacity;
- employees are people, not decorative company stats.

System dependencies:

- profession/capability canon;
- employee/world actor presentation later.

## Minute 7–15 — “One Small Thing”

Player picks up and walks the first meaningful delivery to Mirela.

The route teaches only what is needed to succeed.

The destination should feel like a business location rather than a marker.

System dependencies:

- causal order/cargo/custody;
- valid walking route;
- Work Capacity consume path;
- merchant identity.

## Minute 15–20 — “Somebody Notices”

Mirela gives a brief human reaction. Ana acknowledges the result as professional evidence, not heroic praise.

The player receives the next opportunity because they are now part of a working relationship.

The desired emotional state is:

**“This city contains people who will remember whether I am useful.”**

# 5. First Hour Narrative Beats

The first hour should contain a complete mini-arc with setup, first competence, first pressure, first relationship and first forward-looking choice.

## Beat 1 — A Place to Start

Location: Old Town -> Station Commons.

Purpose:

- establish modest living situation;
- establish immediate need for wages;
- physically move the player into working life.

No scripted debt or fake balance should be added. Living-cost pressure must come from the Personal Economy domain.

## Beat 2 — The First Standard

Location: Station Commons.

Ana explains one professional rule that matters immediately, such as custody/reliability.

Narrative function:

The player is treated as inexperienced, not incompetent.

## Beat 3 — One Small Thing

Location: Station Commons -> Brăila Commerce.

First real delivery to Mirela.

Narrative function:

A small task has a visible business reason.

## Beat 4 — A Person, Not an Address

Location: Old Town.

The player handles a second simple delivery/hand-off involving Petru Neagu or another household customer.

Narrative function:

Contrast merchant service with household service. Introduce the idea that different customers value different things: speed, communication, care, consistency.

## Beat 5 — Capacity Has a Cost

Location: route / Station Commons.

Radu’s workload and the player’s own Work Capacity expose finite human capacity.

The mission system may later present a bounded choice such as:

- take an additional legitimate assignment if capacity/time permits;
- decline without being treated as moral failure;
- help a coworker through a valid company process where implemented.

Narrative function:

“More work” is not always automatically better.

## Beat 6 — First Consequence

A previous delivery outcome produces a small follow-up:

- Mirela requests the same courier/company again after reliable service; or
- she raises concern after a poor outcome; or
- Ana assigns corrective/recovery work after an error.

The consequence must derive from real history rather than a prewritten success flag detached from gameplay.

## Beat 7 — First Pay Means Something

When the authoritative shift/wage rules permit settlement, the player sees that wages came from real work and employer funds.

The emotional function is not “number goes up.”

The player understands that the money now competes with:

- living stability;
- food/basic needs where implemented;
- saving for equipment;
- future training.

No fixed amount is narrative canon.

## Beat 8 — Tomorrow Has More Than One Direction

The first hour closes with one or more visible aspirations:

- Radu talks about bicycle/technical progression;
- Ana mentions that reliable workers may qualify for different responsibilities;
- the phone exposes a training/work information surface only if the underlying feature exists;
- Foundry Quarter or Canal & Quays is framed as a place the player cannot fully operate yet.

The player should finish thinking:

**“I have a job today, but I can become something else.”**

# 6. First 10 Hours

The first ten hours are an approximate playtime arc. They may stretch or compress with player behavior. Story progression must gate on authoritative facts, not on a hidden timer that ignores what the player actually did.

## Hour 0–1 — The First Day

Core beats:

- Ana, Radu, Mirela and Petru introduced;
- first useful work;
- first relationship memory;
- first understanding of Personal Money and Work Capacity;
- first glimpse of career growth.

Emotional state:

Curiosity -> first competence.

## Hour 1–2 — Becoming Familiar

Core beats:

- repeated routes reveal Brăila district identity;
- customers/merchants begin recognizing the player;
- the player learns that reliable work produces opportunity, not just score;
- Ana starts reducing tutorial-like supervision.

Potential systems:

- reputation/customer history;
- recurring demand;
- district traversal;
- phone messages.

Emotional state:

Discovery -> belonging.

## Hour 2–3 — The First Mistake or Recovery

The campaign should expose a recoverable problem.

Possible systemic sources:

- missed time window;
- insufficient Work Capacity;
- route error;
- cargo handling failure;
- merchant/customer dissatisfaction;
- assignment declined because requirements were not met.

The story must support both:

- player-caused failure;
- system-created difficulty that the player handles correctly.

Ana’s response should distinguish dishonesty from honest error.

Emotional state:

Competence tested.

## Hour 3–4 — “What Could I Learn?”

Elena Dobre enters the story through a training/profession opportunity.

The player learns that progression may require:

- theory;
- practical training;
- supervised experience;
- money;
- equipment;
- employer support;
- facility access.

No qualification is granted by dialogue.

Possible early aspiration:

- Bicycle Operation;
- better local delivery capability;
- warehouse/dispatch/technical path preview.

Emotional state:

Capability becomes a choice.

## Hour 4–5 — A Rival With a Reason

Victor Lupu is introduced through legitimate competitive overlap.

Possible framing:

- his company wins a merchant account because it had better capacity or reliability;
- he handles a route the player wanted;
- he criticizes Northstar’s weakness accurately;
- he helps prevent a shared failure because competition does not erase common interest.

The player should understand:

Victor is not “the bad guy.” He is another professional trying to build a future.

Emotional state:

Pride, comparison, ambition.

## Hour 5–6 — “Before the Parcel”

Daria Iancu introduces a production/inventory problem in Foundry Quarter.

Narrative purpose:

Reveal that logistics work is caused by upstream production and downstream need.

Example causal chain, only when supported by #419:

- a productive node lacks an input or has output waiting;
- inventory state creates real supply/demand;
- a logistics opportunity exists because stock must move;
- Mirela or another merchant is affected downstream.

The player sees the world before the pickup marker.

Emotional state:

The city begins to look like a system.

## Hour 6–7 — Personal Pressure

Living costs, equipment goals and Work Capacity create a real personal trade-off.

Possible choices:

- save for training;
- save for bicycle/equipment;
- stabilize ordinary living needs;
- accept additional legitimate work;
- rest and protect future capacity.

The campaign should never present poverty as punishment for not grinding enough.

Recovery paths remain visible.

Emotional state:

Stability is earned, not guaranteed.

## Hour 7–8 — First Career Fork

The player receives meaningful but non-final direction.

Possible branches:

- deeper courier responsibility;
- bicycle path;
- dispatch/operations exposure;
- warehouse exposure;
- technical/maintenance exposure;
- another employer opportunity;
- longer-term entrepreneurship preparation.

Choosing one should not permanently erase all others, but it may delay or change future opportunities.

Emotional state:

Identity begins to form.

## Hour 8–9 — The City Depends on Connections

A multi-party local chain connects at least three narrative actors/places.

Example:

Daria’s output -> logistics movement -> Mirela’s business -> household/customer consequence.

The actual chain must be generated or validated by real inventory/order/custody state.

Ana/Irina may contextualize why the chain matters.

Emotional state:

Responsibility expands beyond one delivery.

## Hour 9–10 — Act I Capstone: “A Chain of Small Things”

Narrative target:

A local disruption threatens several ordinary commitments at once. It is important because the player now knows the people affected.

Possible systemic causes:

- short inventory;
- production interruption;
- insufficient transport capacity;
- staffing shortage;
- infrastructure delay;
- employer financial pressure.

The player’s contribution may involve field work, prioritization, specialist action or cooperation depending on current career capability.

Outcomes may include:

- full recovery;
- partial recovery with one lost customer/contract;
- competitor cooperation;
- Northstar losing business but preserving a critical relationship;
- the player’s personal reputation rising while company performance still struggles;
- a failed outcome that creates a later comeback thread.

No outcome requires magic success.

Act I closes when the player can name people, places and systems that are now different because of what happened.

Emotional state:

**“My work is part of something larger.”**

# 7. Act I — Earning Trust

## Narrative Objective

Transform the player from anonymous new hire into a known local professional.

## Required Story Proofs

Before Act I is considered narratively complete, the player should have experienced:

- at least one repeated customer/merchant relationship;
- at least one recoverable setback;
- at least one career/training aspiration;
- at least one coworker relationship;
- at least one competitor interaction;
- at least one real upstream/downstream economic chain;
- at least one choice where limited time/money/Work Capacity matters;
- at least one visible or persistent consequence in Brăila.

## Exit Condition

Act II opens because the player has options, not merely because a level number was reached.

---

# 8. Act II — Choices

## Narrative Objective

Make opportunity cost personal.

## Core Threads

### Loyalty vs Opportunity

Northstar may offer responsibility while another employer/path offers better specialization or pay.

The player may stay, leave or return later.

### Skill vs Immediate Income

Training may require time, money, supervised practice or temporary lower earnings.

### Transport Choice

Walking remains valid for some work while bicycle/vehicle paths change range/capacity/cost.

### Rivalry vs Cooperation

Victor’s company may compete on contracts but share interest in functioning public infrastructure or emergency supply recovery.

### Professional Identity

The player may begin to be known as reliable courier, technical learner, dispatcher candidate, warehouse operator, manager candidate or entrepreneur-in-preparation.

## Exit Condition

Act III opens when the player has both enough capability and enough history to choose a more independent role.

---

# 9. Act III — Independence

## Narrative Objective

Make the player define success for themselves.

## Employee/Specialist Route

Milestones may include:

- trusted specialist status;
- leading difficult assignments;
- mentoring newer workers;
- operational authority;
- advanced qualification;
- responsibility for equipment/facility/capacity.

## Manager/Dispatcher Route

Milestones may include:

- planning a shift;
- balancing workforce capacity;
- recovering from disruption;
- taking responsibility for team outcomes;
- being judged by both workers and customers.

## Founder Route

Company formation is only offered when #438 prerequisites are truly satisfied.

Narrative sequence:

1. entrepreneurship capability exists;
2. legitimate startup funding exists;
3. local authorization/capacity opportunity exists;
4. required equipment/specialist capability exists;
5. player chooses to risk personal stability;
6. company is registered through the fictional in-world process;
7. a distinct Company Money treasury is created through authoritative settlement;
8. a physical starter operation exists;
9. first customer/employee/failure becomes the company’s actual history.

No “Congratulations, you are CEO” cutscene may bypass these facts.

## Non-Founder Parity Rule

A player who never forms a company must still receive:

- major decisions;
- relationships;
- advancement;
- visible world impact;
- late-game influence;
- legacy.

---

# 10. Act IV — Something Bigger

## Narrative Objective

Move from personal excellence to system responsibility.

## Story Domains

- production chains;
- warehouses/inventory;
- local employment;
- customer-market competition;
- company distress/restructuring;
- training pipelines;
- infrastructure;
- district-level economic activity;
- resilient logistics design.

## Example Arc Family — “The Foundry Week”

A Foundry Quarter production network becomes unstable through legitimate shortages/capacity problems.

Possible player roles:

- courier moves critical small inputs;
- warehouse specialist reallocates stock;
- technician restores equipment/facility capacity;
- dispatcher reprioritizes routes;
- manager negotiates operational trade-offs;
- founder/company competes for or fulfills contracts;
- infrastructure operator improves bottleneck capacity.

Same story pressure; different valid professions.

Outcome persists in business/production history rather than as a universal binary “district saved” flag.

---

# 11. Act V — Beyond Brăila

## Narrative Objective

Make geographic expansion feel earned and emotionally connected to the opening city.

## Romania Expansion

The first regional journeys should originate in real Brăila relationships.

Examples:

- Daria needs a supplier outside the city;
- Northstar assigns regional work to a qualified operator;
- a player company wins a regional contract;
- a warehouse link connects Brăila to another locality;
- river/rail/road gateway capacity becomes relevant.

The player should understand why they are leaving Brăila and what will return because of the journey.

## European Expansion

Later expansion may involve:

- cross-border trade abstractions;
- multimodal hubs;
- partnerships/competition;
- international production networks;
- infrastructure investment;
- organization-scale management.

No map unlock alone equals narrative expansion.

---

# 12. Late Game — Influence and Legacy

## Narrative Objective

Answer:

**What remains because this player lived and worked in this World Instance?**

## Possible Legacy Forms

### Professional Legacy

- trained workers;
- respected qualification path;
- operating standard;
- major recovered network;
- historic service record.

### Organizational Legacy

- durable company;
- founder history;
- successful restructuring;
- ethical/reliable culture;
- long-running customer/employee relationships.

### Infrastructure Legacy

- warehouse/terminal/network;
- fair-access corridor;
- resilient regional connection;
- logistics capacity that outlives one role.

### Economic Legacy

- recovered production cluster;
- stable supplier network;
- regional trade connection;
- investment/governance history.

### Historical Legacy

- Economic Museum/history entry;
- Company Heart / Founder Artifact if applicable;
- records of major company/world events;
- recurring characters reflecting on the player’s earlier choices.

Legacy is world-local economic/history state unless explicitly classified otherwise.

# 14. Scripted vs Emergent Beat Rules

## Scripted Beats Are Appropriate When

- introducing a core recurring character;
- framing the first day;
- explaining a major career decision;
- recognizing a company-formation milestone;
- marking an act transition;
- recording a major legacy/history moment.

## Emergent Beats Are Required When

The drama claims a system event occurred, including:

- shortage;
- company financial distress;
- missed production;
- customer loss;
- staff capacity problem;
- infrastructure bottleneck;
- market/contract outcome;
- actual insolvency;
- company failure/recovery.

A script may contextualize these events but must not fake them.

## Hybrid Beat Pattern

Preferred sequence:

1. authoritative system detects event;
2. narrative resolver identifies affected known characters/places;
3. one recurring character frames the stakes;
4. mission system exposes valid actions based on current capability/state;
5. player acts or declines;
6. authoritative systems settle outcomes;
7. narrative records/reflects what actually happened.

# 15. Persistent Narrative Consequence Model

Future runtime implementation should preserve at least these conceptual fact families.

This is not a required TypeScript schema.

## Relationship Facts

Examples:

- Mirela experienced repeated reliable service from hero/company;
- Ana recommended hero for responsibility;
- Victor and hero cooperated on a shared problem;
- Radu was mentored by hero or vice versa.

## Career Facts

Examples:

- first employer;
- profession history;
- major qualification milestones;
- first supervisory responsibility;
- return to employment after company failure.

## Company Facts

Examples:

- founder identity;
- first employee/customer;
- major contract won/lost;
- restructuring;
- acquisition;
- closure/recovery;
- major infrastructure/project history.

## World Facts

Examples:

- production network recovered;
- warehouse opened;
- public-access infrastructure improved;
- major regional route established;
- historical economic event recorded.

## Persistence Rules

- facts are World-Instance-local by default;
- facts reference stable entity/event identities;
- facts describe settled history, not pending economic mutations;
- narrative facts never become a second money/inventory/capability ledger;
- recovery may add new history without deleting the earlier failure;
- different World Instances may create different later facts from the same canonical opening.

# 16. Mission Writing Rules for Agent 9 and Future Content

Every authored mission should provide the mission-runtime owner with narrative intent, not a bespoke state machine.

Minimum narrative specification:

- **human/economic cause:** why the work exists;
- **affected character/place:** who cares and where;
- **authoritative prerequisites:** capability, time, cargo, employment, infrastructure, etc.;
- **player choice:** what meaningful decision exists;
- **failure state:** what can go wrong;
- **recovery/counterplay:** how play can continue;
- **settlement owner:** which system owns money/inventory/reputation/capability changes;
- **memory:** what characters/world should remember afterward;
- **career compatibility:** how different valid roles can engage or why the mission is intentionally role-specific.

Forbidden mission pattern:

`spawn random parcel -> reach waypoint -> create money -> forget event`.

Preferred mission pattern:

`real need -> real constraint -> known people/places -> valid work/custody -> system settlement -> remembered consequence`.

# 17. Android-First Narrative Presentation Rule

Narrative implementation is subject to #317.

The first-hour story must not become:

- giant modal text dumps;
- debug mission logs;
- tiny unreadable dialogue over the game;
- static developer panels that hide the city.

Preferred mobile-landscape presentation uses a combination of:

- short in-world phone messages;
- brief contextual conversations;
- character/world animation where available;
- environmental changes;
- concise mission framing;
- clear consequences after action.

A player should spend the opening moving through and observing Brăila, not reading a design document inside the game.

# 18. Campaign Acceptance Tests — Design Level

Before a future narrative implementation is considered faithful, the owner should be able to answer yes to the following.

## First 20 Minutes

- Do I know why the player needs the job?
- Have I met at least two memorable people?
- Did the first delivery matter to someone?
- Does Brăila feel like a place rather than a board?
- Is Northstar clearly my employer rather than my company?

## First Hour

- Did somebody remember something I did?
- Did finite money/time/Work Capacity matter?
- Did I see at least one future career direction?
- Did a setback or constraint feel recoverable?
- Did I care about more than the payment?

## First 10 Hours

- Do I recognize recurring people and districts?
- Have I seen real economic cause behind logistics work?
- Have I made at least one meaningful career/opportunity choice?
- Have I encountered a fair rival rather than a cartoon villain?
- Has some visible or persistent part of Brăila changed because of play?

## Long Term

- Can I reach influence without becoming a founder?
- If I become a founder, did it feel earned and risky?
- Can company/world history diverge between World Instances?
- Do failures create comeback stories rather than dead ends?
- Can I describe my history as more than “I completed many deliveries”?

---

# Canonical Campaign Rule

**The DROPi Tycoon campaign begins with one ordinary worker in Brăila and uses recurring people, known places and authoritative economic/logistics consequences to turn personal survival into competence, competence into choice, choice into responsibility, responsibility into systems, and systems into influence and legacy. The guided spine must remain compatible with professional employment, specialization, management, entrepreneurship, infrastructure and investment paths, while different World Instances are free to create different later histories from the same modest beginning.**
