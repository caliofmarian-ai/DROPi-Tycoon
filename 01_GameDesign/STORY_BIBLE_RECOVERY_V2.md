## Core premise

DROPi Tycoon follows an ordinary person who reaches a personal and economic low point and rebuilds life through useful work, relationships, learning, entrepreneurship and cooperation.

The hero is not chosen, famous or secretly wealthy. Importance is earned.

The emotional promise remains:

> **Your work leaves a mark.**

The story grows from one person needing one safe night into human organizations whose work can eventually shape cities, regions and global networks.

## Player identity

At onboarding the player chooses hero presentation sex:

- `Male`
- `Female`

The story, missions, money, XP, qualifications, specialist access, company access and multiplayer progression are identical. Presentation/localization may select grammatical forms such as father/mother (`tată` / `mamă`). Sex selection never changes professional or economic eligibility.

The common opening history is:

- the hero has gone through a divorce/separation;
- the hero's children are not living beside them at the opening;
- the loss of family stability contributed to severe personal/economic decline;
- the hero is sleeping rough with almost no financial margin;
- the hero wants to become a parent their children can be proud of.

The game does not assign blame for the separation or invent custody law. Homelessness is portrayed with dignity as a temporary condition, not as a joke or permanent identity.

## Chapter 0 — I Have to Get Up

The opening is a short cinematic/gameplay braid.

Required presentation:

1. cold evening, night or early morning;
2. worn inexpensive clothes;
3. no professional equipment, vehicle, company or office;
4. modest improvised sleeping place using cardboard/shelter material;
5. exhaustion and fear shown without gratuitous misery;
6. short internal monologue;
7. control returns to the same hero in the same world.

Canonical monologue meaning:

> I have to get up. I have to keep going. My children are not beside me anymore, but I still have to become the parent they can be proud of. I have to get out of this. First, I need work.

First playable objective:

> **Walk the streets and look for work opportunities.**

Movement is therefore learned through a real story need rather than a detached tutorial.

## The first human door — Maria in Brăila

The portable role is `opening-merchant`. Brăila binds that role to **Maria**, a local greengrocer/farm-products owner-operator. Maria is not universal outside Brăila.

Brăila stable identity: `maria-ionescu`.

Maria is practical, humane, observant, skeptical of promises and loyal to people who prove reliable. She helps without inventing a job that does not exist.

The hero notices customers, fruit, vegetables and local farm goods and thinks, in substance:

> This place has customers. Maybe she needs help here. If not, maybe the farm she buys from does. I have to ask.

The hero physically enters Maria's shop interior and speaks with her.

Maria does not hire the hero. She offers one legitimate delivery as a test.

Canonical Maria meaning:

> I do not have a job for you here. But if you want to show me I can rely on you, take this delivery. Get it to the right person, intact and on time. Then come back.

The parcel/goods are visibly handed to the hero and remain visibly carried through pickup -> travel -> handoff. Dialogue never substitutes for authoritative cargo custody.

## The first return

After the first delivery the player returns to Maria.

Maria checks that the recipient received it, acknowledges the result and may provide a small in-kind food reward such as apples when inventory/economy authority supports it.

Canonical Maria meaning:

> You came back, and the customer confirmed it. Good. Take these. You earned something today — and you look like you need to eat.

Maria then offers a bounded Brăila trial chain of approximately five legitimate deliveries.

> Do these properly and I will speak to one of the farmers I buy from. I cannot promise you a job. I can promise I will tell him whether you are reliable.

This establishes the first relationship loop:

`small chance -> useful work -> remembered outcome -> another door opens`.

## Learning the economy through deliveries

Early deliveries deliberately touch different legitimate endpoints, for example:

- household customer;
- shop;
- restaurant/food service;
- accommodation/hospitality;
- farm/producer;
- warehouse/processing site;
- another local service business.

A legitimate settlement may produce only consequences authorized by owning systems:

`money/wage consequence + player XP + entity loyalty + eligible Specialist Card Fragments + eligible self-study/exposure progress`.

Specialist Card Fragments do not grant qualification, employment or specialist capability. Governed completed sets may help discover/recruit a specialist profile. Self-study/exposure does not replace formal or supervised qualification where required.

## The supplier/farm door

After Maria's trial chain, her recommendation opens a local producer/supplier meeting.

The farmer has heard the hero is fast/reliable but has no suitable permanent vacancy now. A future role may become possible as the business expands, but advanced work requires real capability/qualification.

The farmer therefore offers legitimate temporary deliveries/tasks that the hero is actually eligible to perform and encourages the hero to ask partner businesses about work.

Narrative rule:

> **Goodwill opens a conversation. Capability decides which work is actually available.**

## Housing recovery

The earliest nights retain the rough sleeping place while Personal Money is insufficient for stable housing.

Visible progression should be:

`street sleeping -> food/basic recovery -> very modest rented room -> more stable housing -> better housing later`.

The first rented room is deliberately humble. Housing transitions must read/consume authoritative Personal Money and housing state; a cutscene cannot grant free property.

## Founder thought

Repeated temporary work and repeated inability to secure stable employment produce the first entrepreneurial thought.

Canonical meaning:

> Everyone needs something moved, but nobody has a permanent place for me. Maybe one day I build the work myself. A delivery company. Not today. First I need a roof, money, and to understand how this world works.

This is the birth of the DROPi founder aspiration, not an instant company grant.

## Administrative specialist

After basic housing and initial capital, the hero needs a locality-specific business/administrative specialist who explains legitimate registration paths, costs, prerequisites and limits.

In Brăila the existing `mihai-enache` identity may later bind this role after implementation reconciliation.

Personality direction:

- charismatic and hopeful;
- treats small ambitions seriously;
- clear about costs/limitations;
- never invents legal permissions;
- does not give the company for free.

Canonical exchange meaning:

Hero:
> I want to start a delivery business.

Specialist:
> Good. Every large company was once smaller than the idea behind it. Now let us see what you can legally and realistically build with what you have.

Jurisdiction-specific legal forms are local bindings. Romanian `PFA`/`SRL` rules are not universal game constants.

## Starter venture — small by design

The first independent venture teaches:

- Personal Money versus Company Money;
- customers/contracts;
- specialist recruitment;
- wages/costs;
- equipment/capability;
- the limits of doing everything alone.

The starter venture has a deliberately small NPC workforce cap under `EARLY_COMPANY_SOCIAL_SCALING_CONTRACT.md`. This is a gameplay/social-progression cap, not a claim about real employment law.

The player may remain a small operator, but large-company scale requires human collaboration.

## Social gate — real players matter

The canonical early organizational progression is:

`solo recovery -> small venture -> a few NPC specialists -> growth pressure -> meet real players -> create or join a human organization -> larger company capability`.

At the social gate the player can:

1. associate with at least one other real player and create/upgrade into a human company; or
2. join an existing human-led company.

NPCs may fill legitimate jobs but cannot satisfy the real-player membership gate for large-company progression.

The game must provide discovery/matchmaking surfaces so this is a social opportunity, not a dead end. A player who does not want multiplayer may continue as a small operator/career player, but cannot simulate the same large-company tier by replacing every human partner with NPCs.

## Film + gameplay form

DROPi Tycoon deliberately combines:

- cinematic beats for emotional transitions;
- real gameplay for work, movement, cargo, money, learning and company operations;
- short in-world dialogue for relationships;
- visible environmental progression for life recovery;
- systemic economic events for consequence.

A film can establish meaning. It cannot fabricate money, cargo, qualification, housing, employment, company formation or multiplayer membership.

## Early campaign spine

### Chapter 0 — Rise
`rough sleeping -> decision -> search for work`

Question: **Can I get through today?**

### Chapter 1 — One Chance
`Maria -> first test delivery -> return -> food/help -> trial chain`

Question: **Can one person rely on me?**

### Chapter 2 — Doors, Not Jobs
`farm/supplier -> temporary work -> partner businesses -> sector exposure`

Question: **What can I learn while I am still looking for a place?**

### Chapter 3 — A Roof
`earn -> survive -> rent modest room`

Question: **Can work create stability?**

### Chapter 4 — The Idea
`repeated demand -> founder thought -> capital -> administrative specialist`

Question: **Can I create the opportunity nobody has offered me?**

### Chapter 5 — The Small Venture
`registration -> company account -> first customer -> first NPC specialists -> operating costs`

Question: **Can I be responsible for more than myself?**

### Chapter 6 — The Limit of Doing It Alone
`growth -> starter workforce cap -> operational pressure -> need for partners`

Question: **Who can I trust enough to build with?**

### Chapter 7 — Human Company
`invite/join real players -> shared responsibility -> larger workforce/assets/contracts`

Question: **Can we build something that none of us could build alone?**

### Chapter 8+ — City, Region, World
Production, infrastructure, investment, multimodal logistics and international networks unlock only through legitimate capabilities and world state.

## Global portability

The emotional spine is global; the local cast is not.

Portable roles include opening merchant, producer/supplier, household customer, trainer, business-administration specialist, peer/rival, employer/dispatcher and potential human cofounder/member.

Brăila is the premium authored reference. Other supported localities bind their own people, businesses, sectors and legal forms without silently cloning Brăila identities.

Portable spine:

`recovery -> search -> merchant chance -> useful work -> local network -> housing stability -> founder aspiration -> starter venture -> human collaboration`.

## Systems-must-carry-the-drama rule

Dialogue opens doors. It does not mint state.

- food reward requires legitimate inventory/economic consequence;
- delivery requires real order/cargo custody;
- rent requires Personal Money/housing authority;
- qualification requires capability authority;
- specialist fragments remain governed discovery artifacts;
- company formation requires business/economy/legal prerequisites;
- large-company human membership requires multiplayer/identity authority;
- shortages/demand claimed by story must come from real systems where applicable.

## Tone

Grounded, warm, observant and hopeful. The opening may be painful, but the game is about recovery and agency.

Avoid chosen-one framing, villain monologues, constant praise, caricature of poverty/divorce/homelessness, or misery as spectacle.

## Canonical narrative rule

**DROPi Tycoon tells the story of an ordinary parent who begins at a personal and economic low point, gets up, searches for legitimate work, earns trust through useful work, learns the economy by moving through it, rebuilds personal stability, develops the ambition to create a delivery business, starts deliberately small, discovers that organizations are built from real people rather than unlimited NPC substitutes, and eventually creates or joins human companies whose decisions can shape cities, regions and global networks. Cinematic storytelling gives those transitions emotion; authoritative gameplay makes them true.**
