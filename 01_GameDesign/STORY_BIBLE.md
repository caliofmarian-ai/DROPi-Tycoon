# DROPi Tycoon Story Bible

## Purpose

This document defines the canonical narrative identity of DROPi Tycoon: what the game is about emotionally, who the player meets, how Brăila becomes memorable, how the guided story grows with the simulation, and how persistent choices can create different histories in different World Instances.

Authority order remains:

`00_Project/VISION.md` -> `00_Project/UNIVERSE_DESIGN.md` -> `00_Project/BUSINESS_DESIGN.md` -> `00_Project/LOGISTICS_DESIGN.md` -> `01_GameDesign/GDD.md` -> `01_GameDesign/STORY_BIBLE.md` -> narrative implementation.

This document specializes existing canon. It does not create a second economy, mission runtime, map, progression system, or multiplayer authority model.

Detailed campaign pacing and dependency gates are in `01_GameDesign/CAMPAIGN_STRUCTURE.md`.

# 1. The Answer to “What Is DROPi Tycoon About?”

DROPi Tycoon is the story of an ordinary person who starts with almost nothing and gradually becomes someone whose work matters to other people and to the world around them.

The player begins poor, on foot, with a basic smartphone and a job at a fictional incumbent logistics company in Brăila. At first, success means arriving on time, carrying the right thing, treating people well, surviving the week, and proving that somebody can trust you with one more responsibility.

Over time, that same human may become a skilled courier, dispatcher, technician, warehouse specialist, manager, founder, producer, infrastructure operator, investor, or another legitimate participant in the economy. The player may remain an employee for an entire career and still reach high influence and legacy. Entrepreneurship is a major possible life event, not a mandatory promotion.

As the player grows, the scale of consequence grows with them:

- one customer depends on one delivery;
- one merchant depends on reliable supply;
- one team depends on good decisions;
- one company depends on people, cash flow, assets, inventory and trust;
- one district depends on functioning economic relationships;
- one region depends on transport and production networks;
- eventually, cities and countries become connected by systems the player helped build, repair, operate or influence.

The emotional promise is the existing project promise:

> **Your work leaves a mark.**

The narrative corollary is:

> **Every route can become a relationship. Every relationship can become a network. Every network can become history.**

# 2. Narrative Pillars

## 2.1 Ordinary Beginning, Earned Importance

The player is not a chosen one, secret heir, celebrity, combat hero, or preselected genius.

The player becomes important because they repeatedly learn, work, make choices, accept responsibility, recover from setbacks, and create useful capability.

No plot beat may grant unlimited money, qualifications, vehicles, companies, infrastructure or access merely because the story wants a dramatic moment.

## 2.2 Human Stakes Before Global Stakes

The player should care about a person before being asked to care about a market index.

Early narrative stakes therefore use recognizable pressures:

- a merchant waiting for stock;
- a customer who has been disappointed before;
- a coworker covering too many routes;
- a dispatcher trying to keep a difficult shift functioning;
- a player trying to afford ordinary life;
- a business that may lose customers after repeated late service;
- a training opportunity the player cannot yet afford or qualify for.

Larger economic stakes are introduced only after the player understands the people and places inside those systems.

## 2.3 Conflict Without Combat

DROPi Tycoon narrative conflict is primarily economic, logistical, social, professional and institutional.

Strong conflicts include:

- limited time and Work Capacity;
- conflicting commitments;
- late or damaged cargo;
- unreliable supply;
- production stoppage;
- employer cash pressure;
- difficult customers;
- staff shortages;
- career decisions;
- loyalty versus opportunity;
- fair competition;
- company failure and recovery;
- infrastructure bottlenecks;
- neighborhood decline or recovery;
- ethical choices around service, labor, pricing, access, responsibility and risk.

Competitors should normally be understandable people or organizations with legitimate motives, not cartoon villains.

## 2.4 Systems Must Carry the Drama

A narrative beat may only claim a consequence that the authoritative gameplay system can eventually represent.

Examples:

- a wage is a Company Money -> Personal Money settlement, not a quest faucet;
- exhaustion is Work Capacity pressure, not arbitrary screen shake;
- a better job depends on capability, employer permission and opportunity, not a cutscene promotion;
- a shortage comes from inventory/supply-demand state, not a scripted text box pretending stock is missing;
- a company crisis must connect to actual money, workforce, inventory, contracts, reputation, capacity or infrastructure;
- a district recovery should become visible through actual world/economic state where feasible.

When a required system is not implemented, the beat remains a future narrative gate and must not be presented as already live.

## 2.5 Recovery Is Part of the Story

Failure is not a narrative dead end.

A player may become poor, lose housing stability, lose a job, fail a company, lose company control, miss a contract, damage a relationship, or be forced into a lower-responsibility role.

The person and their legitimate history remain.

A comeback can become one of the strongest stories in the game.

# 3. Canonical Player Starting Situation

The canonical fresh-world hero is one world-local human economic actor.

The player may choose or customize personal presentation and other non-economic identity details where supported. Narrative canon does not require a fixed gender, fixed name, fixed family history, or fixed birthplace.

The situation is canonical:

- the story begins in Brăila;
- the player has almost no disposable Personal Money;
- walking is the available transport mode;
- the player owns or has access only to basic personal clothing, a backpack/basic carrying capability and a basic smartphone;
- the player is newly employed by the fictional incumbent logistics employer;
- the player does not own that employer;
- the player does not own a private HQ;
- the player has only introductory delivery capability;
- the player has one short opening grace period of basic shelter/stability, after which ordinary living costs matter;
- the player needs productive work because economic stability is not guaranteed.

The opening does not require the game to decide whether the hero was born in Brăila or recently arrived. The meaningful truth is that **this is where the player’s economic life begins**.

The player’s first ambition is deliberately small: complete today’s work, earn trust, and create one more option for tomorrow.

# 4. The Opening Employer

The opening employer is the large fictional incumbent logistics company currently represented in the employee-first domain as **Northstar Parcel Logistics**.

Narrative identity:

- it is already established when the player begins;
- it has real employees, costs, schedules, customers and operational pressure;
- it is neither evil nor benevolent by default;
- it provides the player’s first productive rung in the economy;
- it can become a long-term career home, a place the player outgrows, a future competitor, a partner, or an institution the player later helps reform;
- employment does not imply membership, ownership or executive authority.

The stable narrative entity is more important than a specific implementation constant. If a future brand/name migration is approved, character relationships and company history must migrate with the same entity rather than creating a new employer.

Northstar must never become an infinite-money tutorial company. Its ability to pay wages, accept work and compete should increasingly obey the same constrained economy as other organizations.

# 5. Brăila Is a Character

Brăila is not a disposable tutorial map. It is the player’s first social memory, first economic laboratory and first proof that a place can change.

Narrative uses the established gameplay geography and does not modify map geometry.

## Old Town

Narrative identity: intimacy, households, early customers, narrow margins, routine, memory.

This is where the player most strongly learns that a delivery ends with a person, not a waypoint.

Typical stories:

- repeat customers;
- promises and missed promises;
- elderly or mobility-limited residents without turning them into helpless stereotypes;
- small household needs;
- early housing/stability context;
- neighborhood trust.

## Brăila Commerce

Narrative identity: merchants, queues, deadlines, cash flow, local opportunity.

This is where one delayed parcel can become a business problem and where the player first sees the difference between “delivery completed” and “customer actually served.”

Typical stories:

- small merchants;
- recurring pickup relationships;
- stock replenishment;
- customer reputation;
- price/service pressure;
- competing carriers.

## Foundry Quarter

Narrative identity: production, storage, repair, physical work, industrial resilience.

This district becomes the early bridge from last-mile delivery into the larger stock-flow economy.

Typical stories:

- packaging and small manufacturing;
- inventory shortages;
- maintenance;
- warehouse and technical careers;
- production stoppages;
- later industrial investment or employment.

## Station Commons

Narrative identity: work, movement, training, dispatch, crossroads.

This is the natural narrative home of the opening depot/work hub, later training opportunities, transport transitions and career decisions.

Typical stories:

- shift starts;
- dispatcher conversations;
- coworker relationships;
- route assignment;
- training access;
- bicycle/vehicle progression;
- regional transport gateways.

## Canal & Quays

Narrative identity: Brăila’s relationship with the Danube, trade, continuity, larger horizons.

This district should foreshadow regional and international logistics long before the player can operate them.

Typical stories:

- river-side businesses;
- older residents with memories of changing trade patterns;
- cargo awaiting onward movement;
- infrastructure constraints;
- future port/fluvial logistics.

## Garden Borough

Narrative identity: growth, public life, households, the visible quality of a city.

This district provides a counterweight to industrial/logistics spaces and later becomes useful for stories about urban development, access, public services and local quality of life.

## Geographic Rule

Narrative may associate characters and events with these established zones and with source-backed runtime streets/addresses.

Narrative must not invent a parallel fictional street grid or require geometry changes. Real OSM-backed street names remain runtime/world authority.

# 6. Main Recurring Cast

The initial cast is intentionally small. Characters recur across work, career and world-scale growth so the player develops history with them.

No character is guaranteed to remain in the same job forever. Their later role may diverge by World Instance.

## 6.1 Ana Stoica — First Dispatcher / Mentor

**Stable role:** opening dispatcher and first professional mentor at Northstar Parcel Logistics.

**Personality:** precise, dryly funny, observant, impatient with excuses but not with honest inexperience.

**Motivation:** keep the shift reliable without sacrificing people to impossible promises. She wants the depot to become competent enough that every problem does not require emergency improvisation.

**Relationship to player:** first person to give the hero responsibility and first person whose trust must be earned repeatedly.

**Relationship to world/economy:** lives inside schedules, capacity, customer commitments, staffing limits and operational trade-offs.

**Potential arc:**

- strict supervisor -> trusted mentor -> professional peer;
- may recommend the player for training or responsibility;
- may disagree with a founder/player who later prioritizes growth over reliability;
- may remain at Northstar, move into management, or join another organization depending on world history.

**Gameplay systems:** employment, Work Capacity, shifts, assignment, dispatch, reputation, management, company performance.

## 6.2 Radu Marin — Coworker / Friend / Friendly Competitor

**Stable role:** another early courier whose life advances alongside the player’s.

**Personality:** sociable, resourceful, proud, sometimes too eager to prove himself.

**Motivation:** escape permanent financial insecurity and build a technical career rather than remain trapped in the least-skilled work.

**Relationship to player:** first peer relationship. He can become a friend, friendly rival, colleague, employee, contractor or distant professional contact.

**Relationship to world/economy:** experiences the same wage pressure, training gates, equipment choices and career uncertainty as the player.

**Potential arc:**

- walking courier -> bicycle courier -> maintenance/technical or fleet path;
- may make a bad equipment purchase, miss training, or recover through supervised work;
- can demonstrate that NPC careers evolve without orbiting the player.

**Gameplay systems:** professions, qualifications, equipment, employment, wages, technical/maintenance paths.

## 6.3 Mirela Stan — Merchant / First Recurring Business Customer

**Stable role:** owner-operator of a small independent commerce business serving local customers and preparing orders for pickup/delivery.

**Personality:** practical, fast-thinking, skeptical of corporate promises, loyal to people who consistently deliver.

**Motivation:** keep a fragile local business reliable enough to survive inconsistent supply and larger competitors.

**Relationship to player:** the first merchant who remembers the hero by name/identity because the hero’s reliability has direct effect on her day.

**Relationship to world/economy:** demand, inventory, customer satisfaction, supplier reliability, local commerce.

**Potential arc:**

- suspicious customer -> recurring client -> strategic account;
- may expand, specialize, struggle, switch carriers, or become part of a wider local supply network;
- if the player later runs a company, she judges it by service, not nostalgia.

**Gameplay systems:** merchants, orders, inventory/demand, customer relationships, contracts, local competition.

## 6.4 Petru Neagu — Recurring Household Customer / City Memory

**Stable role:** Old Town resident and repeat customer.

**Personality:** independent, curious, sharp-eyed, slow to give praise.

**Motivation:** maintain control over ordinary life and remain connected to a city that keeps changing around him.

**Relationship to player:** initially one demanding stop among many; later a human reference point for how the player and city have changed.

**Relationship to world/economy:** household consumption, service reliability, neighborhood continuity, local history.

**Potential arc:**

- dissatisfied recipient -> familiar face -> respected witness to the player’s growth;
- can react differently if the player becomes a distant executive versus remaining a local professional;
- may contribute memories or donated artifacts to future history/museum systems without becoming a magical lore oracle.

**Gameplay systems:** household demand, recurring customers, reputation, world history, Economic Museum/legacy later.

## 6.5 Daria Iancu — Local Producer / Business Owner

**Stable role:** operator of a small packaging/processing business in Foundry Quarter.

**Personality:** analytical, stubborn, transparent about numbers, dislikes dependency on one supplier or carrier.

**Motivation:** keep production running through ordinary shortages, machine downtime and customer pressure.

**Relationship to player:** introduces the idea that logistics begins before pickup and continues after delivery.

**Relationship to world/economy:** production inputs, output inventory, procurement, storage, contracts, local employment.

**Potential arc:**

- occasional shipper -> recurring production client/employer -> regional supplier;
- may scale, restructure, be acquired, cooperate with competitors, or fail and restart;
- can become a major ally or a demanding customer without becoming “the player’s factory.”

**Gameplay systems:** #419 production/inventory/supply-demand, contracts, warehouses, workforce, regional trade.

## 6.6 Elena Dobre — Trainer / Instructor

**Stable role:** instructor at a fictional Brăila vocational/training facility.

**Personality:** calm, demanding, allergic to shortcuts.

**Motivation:** make qualifications mean demonstrated competence rather than purchased badges.

**Relationship to player:** gatekeeper to earned capability, later a professional reference and possible collaborator in workforce development.

**Relationship to world/economy:** training access, supervised experience, facilities, employer-funded or personally funded education.

**Potential arc:**

- instructor -> assessor -> program coordinator;
- may challenge the player’s organization to invest in real training rather than hiring labels;
- may later train the player’s employees or oppose unsafe expansion.

**Gameplay systems:** #437/#359 capabilities, theory, practical training, supervised experience, Personal Money, employer sponsorship.

## 6.7 Victor Lupu — Competitor / Rival Professional

**Stable role:** capable courier/operations figure associated with a competing local logistics company.

**Personality:** ambitious, disciplined, competitive, not needlessly cruel.

**Motivation:** win enough reliable business to protect his own career and team and prove that his organization deserves market share.

**Relationship to player:** rival whose respect is earned through performance. Cooperation is possible when mutual interests align.

**Relationship to world/economy:** customer acquisition, contract competition, capacity, pricing/service trade-offs, workforce competition.

**Potential arc:**

- irritating benchmark -> respected rival -> partner on shared infrastructure or crisis response;
- can become more aggressive if his company is under financial pressure;
- may defeat the player fairly on contracts;
- may later work for, invest in, merge with, or remain independent from a player-founded company depending on history.

**Gameplay systems:** competition, reputation, contracts, company performance, infrastructure cooperation, recovery/counterplay.

## 6.8 Irina Pavel — Operations Planner / Future Colleague or Partner

**Stable role:** analytically minded operations worker introduced after the player understands basic field work.

**Personality:** patient, strategic, curious about systems rather than status.

**Motivation:** prove that good operations design can improve reliability without exploiting workers or wasting capacity.

**Relationship to player:** future colleague who remains relevant across employee, manager and founder paths.

**Relationship to world/economy:** dispatch, routing, capacity planning, warehouse flow, regional operations.

**Potential arc:**

- junior planner -> operations specialist -> manager/partner/independent consultant;
- may work beside the player at Northstar, join a new venture, or become a respected external counterpart;
- provides a narrative bridge from “I can do this job” to “I can design a system that helps many people do it.”

**Gameplay systems:** dispatch, capacity, warehouse/logistics specialization, company growth, regional networks.

## 6.9 Mihai Enache — Institutional Representative

**Stable role:** representative at a fictional local enterprise/mobility service desk used for game-world registration, business authorization and infrastructure-facing progression where those systems exist.

**Personality:** procedural but helpful, values clarity and preparedness.

**Motivation:** keep scarce local opportunities usable, lawful within game abstractions and fair enough that one company cannot permanently lock everyone else out.

**Relationship to player:** not a quest dispenser; he becomes relevant when the player’s choices require civic/business authorization or shared-infrastructure access.

**Relationship to world/economy:** company formation, local capacity, fair-access rules, infrastructure permissions.

**Potential arc:**

- distant official -> recurring institutional counterpart;
- may approve, deny or condition future proposals based on authoritative prerequisites;
- remains neutral enough to deal with the player, Northstar and competitors.

**Gameplay systems:** #438 company formation, territorial capacity, institutions, infrastructure/fair access.

# 7. Relationship Design Rules

Relationships are neither a hidden morality meter nor a single universal friendship score.

Important relationships should remember concrete categories of behavior, such as:

- reliability;
- honesty;
- professional competence;
- promises kept or broken;
- fair treatment;
- willingness to help under legitimate cost;
- competitive conduct;
- mentorship/support;
- harmful decisions;
- recovery/apology where possible.

A character may respect the player professionally without liking a business decision. A merchant may like the player personally but switch to a competing company after repeated service failures.

Relationships must not override authoritative economic rules. Friendship cannot conjure inventory, qualifications, money, company ownership or infrastructure access.

# 8. Story Architecture

## Prologue — The First Day

Theme: **“Can anybody rely on me?”**

The player begins at human scale with one day of stability and very few options.

The prologue establishes:

- the player’s constrained starting situation;
- the smartphone as a work/navigation/message tool;
- Northstar Parcel Logistics as employer, not owned company;
- Ana as dispatcher;
- Radu as peer;
- Mirela as the first merchant who turns a delivery into a relationship;
- one Old Town household/customer connection;
- Brăila as a place with distinct districts and economic roles;
- the principle that a small delivery matters because another person or business is waiting for it.

The emotional finish is not “tutorial complete.” It is **“I was useful today, and somebody noticed.”**

## Act I — Earning Trust

Theme: **competence becomes reputation.**

The player learns Brăila by repeatedly serving people and businesses who remember previous outcomes.

Core pressures:

- Work Capacity;
- basic living costs;
- reliability;
- route knowledge;
- small equipment goals;
- difficult but understandable customers;
- coworker dependence;
- first mistakes and recoveries.

Act I ends when the player is no longer anonymous. Multiple people and organizations now have a reason to request, recommend, avoid or challenge the player based on history.

## Act II — Choices

Theme: **every opportunity closes or delays another.**

The player gains enough stability to make meaningful choices rather than only survive.

Possible choices include:

- remain with Northstar or consider another employer;
- pursue bicycle/vehicle capability;
- train for dispatch, warehouse, maintenance or another profession;
- prioritize income now versus training later;
- accept difficult assignments that build reputation but consume capacity;
- form stronger loyalty to particular customers, coworkers or districts;
- compete with Victor’s organization or cooperate where appropriate.

Act II should make the player feel that a career is forming.

## Act III — Independence

Theme: **“What kind of working life do I want?”**

Independence does not mean “become CEO.”

Valid Act III identities include:

- highly trusted career employee;
- specialist operator;
- dispatcher/operations professional;
- technician/maintenance professional;
- warehouse/logistics specialist;
- manager;
- independent contractor where later authorized;
- entrepreneur/founder;
- producer/merchant path where compatible.

If company formation is chosen, it must feel like a major life event with legitimate capital, capability, authorization and risk. The player does not receive a free company because Act III began.

If company formation is not chosen, the player still receives a complete narrative path with responsibility, influence and legacy.

## Act IV — Something Bigger

Theme: **responsibility for systems, not just tasks.**

The player’s actions now affect connected economic systems:

- multiple employees;
- merchant networks;
- warehouses;
- production chains;
- inventory shortages;
- local competition;
- neighborhood activity;
- infrastructure;
- company recovery/failure;
- training pipelines;
- local employment.

Narrative conflict increasingly asks the player to allocate limited resources and decide whose needs or risks to prioritize.

The player should witness consequences in the city: a recovered supply relationship, a busier facility, a struggling business, a new workshop, a changed route network, a reopened production chain, or another system-backed visible result.

## Act V — Beyond Brăila

Theme: **earned reach.**

Romania opens progressively through legitimate travel, contracts, capability and infrastructure.

Brăila remains emotionally important even after expansion.

The player moves from local identity to regional responsibility:

- inter-city routes;
- regional suppliers/customers;
- warehouses and gateways;
- rail/road/river/air connections as unlocked;
- country-scale competition/cooperation;
- international trade and multimodal logistics later.

Expansion should feel like carrying lessons learned in Brăila into larger systems, not abandoning the “tutorial city.”

## Late Game — Influence & Legacy

Theme: **what remains because I was here?**

Late-game influence may include:

- major infrastructure;
- regional or international networks;
- durable organizations;
- workforce development;
- recovered industries;
- company histories;
- major innovations;
- investment/governance influence;
- professional standards;
- mentorship;
- museum/history recognition;
- founder legacy where relevant.

Legacy is not reserved for founders.

A lifelong dispatcher who designed resilient regional operations, a technician who created a training standard, an infrastructure operator who kept essential access fair, or an employee who became a respected national specialist can all leave major history.

The Company Heart / Founder Artifact may become one optional late-game historical object for a mature company. It is fictional prestige/history material only. It is not blockchain, an NFT, a wallet asset, or a replacement for company history.

# 9. The Epic Thread

The campaign spine is one continuous question that changes scale:

1. **Can anybody rely on me?**
2. **What am I capable of?**
3. **Who do I want to work with and become?**
4. **What am I responsible for now?**
5. **What system am I helping build or repair?**
6. **Who is affected by my decisions?**
7. **What will still exist because of the choices I made?**

This is the epic story of DROPi Tycoon.

It does not require a war, apocalypse, conspiracy, villain empire or supernatural destiny.

The scale becomes epic because the player can eventually connect thousands of ordinary needs through systems they genuinely helped create.

# 10. Career-Path Narrative Compatibility

Every major career family must have a complete emotional progression.

## Professional Employee

Fantasy: become indispensable without owning the company.

Narrative rewards: trust, responsibility, specialist authority, mentoring, better assignments, leadership, history inside an organization.

## Logistics Specialist

Fantasy: master difficult movement/custody problems.

Narrative rewards: complex cargo, multimodal responsibility, high-trust contracts, operational reputation.

## Dispatcher / Manager

Fantasy: make a team/system work reliably.

Narrative rewards: people depending on decisions, capacity trade-offs, recovery from disruption, organizational influence.

## Technician / Maintenance Specialist

Fantasy: keep the physical network alive.

Narrative rewards: visible repairs, restored capacity, training, workshop identity, prevention of larger failures.

## Warehouse / Operations Specialist

Fantasy: turn disorder into dependable flow.

Narrative rewards: throughput, inventory accuracy, shortage recovery, transfer coordination, regional scale.

## Entrepreneur / Founder

Fantasy: risk personal stability to create an organization.

Narrative rewards: first registration, first company treasury, first employee, first customer, first failure, first facility, governance, founder history.

## Infrastructure Operator

Fantasy: build and operate systems many organizations depend on.

Narrative rewards: access, capacity, fairness, resilience, regional development.

## Investor / Business Strategist

Fantasy: influence organizations through analysis, capital and governance rather than personally doing every task.

Narrative rewards: trade-offs, stewardship, governance decisions, restructuring, portfolio history. This is a later-game path and must not become guaranteed-return gameplay.

# 11. Scripted, Emergent and Hybrid Story

DROPi Tycoon uses three narrative forms.

## Scripted Spine

Used for:

- first meeting with core cast;
- opening premise;
- major career-decision framing;
- first company formation if chosen;
- major act transitions;
- selected legacy/history moments.

Scripted scenes establish meaning and character but do not fabricate system state.

## Emergent Story

Generated by authoritative simulation, such as:

- shortage;
- company distress;
- delayed supply;
- customer churn;
- employee availability;
- production interruption;
- market change;
- infrastructure bottleneck;
- failure/recovery;
- World Instance history.

Emergent events should be narrated because they happened, not forced to happen because a script needs drama.

## Hybrid Story

The preferred mature model.

A scripted character recognizes and contextualizes a real systemic event.

Example:

- the production system detects a packaging shortage;
- Mirela’s business demand is affected;
- Daria’s production node lacks an input;
- Ana or Irina explains the operational consequence;
- the player receives only work/contracts that the system can legitimately generate;
- afterward, characters remember the outcome.

The story therefore gains authored emotional meaning without creating false economic truth.

# 12. Persistent Consequence Rules

Narrative consequences must be stable enough to become history but flexible enough to permit recovery.

Persistent consequence categories may include:

- relationship memory;
- employer loyalty/history;
- profession/career history;
- completed training/mentorship relationships;
- customer trust history;
- company founder/history records;
- major contract outcomes;
- company failure/recovery events;
- infrastructure construction/operation history;
- district/production recovery milestones;
- major rivalry/cooperation outcomes;
- legacy/museum records.

Rules:

1. A narrative flag cannot replace authoritative economic state.
2. A systemic consequence should reference stable world/entity/event identities.
3. Exactly-once economic settlements remain owned by their economic domain, not dialogue.
4. Relationship memory may modify future presentation/opportunity only through governed rules.
5. Ordinary failure must have a recovery route.
6. No character can grant core capability merely because they like the player.
7. No scripted scene may duplicate cargo, money, shares, inventory, qualifications or infrastructure.
8. A later retcon must migrate history deliberately rather than silently rewriting it.

---

# 13. World Instance Narrative Rules

The canonical beginning may be shared across fresh World Instances that use the same narrative baseline version.

After that, history may diverge.

## Invariant Starting Facts

Examples:

- hero starts as a poor employee in Brăila;
- Northstar is the opening incumbent employer;
- opening cast identities exist;
- first districts/places have the same baseline roles;
- no mature-world economic power transfers into the new world.

## Divergent History

Examples:

- Mirela’s business may expand in one world and close/restructure in another;
- Radu may become a technician, manager or leave the city;
- Victor may remain a rival, become a partner, or work inside another organization;
- Northstar may dominate, struggle, restructure or cooperate;
- the player may found a company or never do so;
- infrastructure may be built in different orders;
- districts may develop different economic identities.

The story must not script one immutable future that contradicts these possible world histories.

## Narrative Persistence Key

Future runtime narrative state should be World-Instance-local and tied to stable entity/event identities.

It must never use account-only economic history as if it belonged to every world.

Settings, tutorial familiarity and explicitly non-economic account history may be handled separately under World Instance rules.

---

# 14. Tone and Dialogue Rules

Tone is grounded, warm, observant and occasionally humorous.

The world may be difficult without becoming relentlessly bleak.

Dialogue should:

- sound like people who work for a living;
- communicate stakes through concrete consequences;
- avoid exposition dumps about game systems;
- let characters disagree intelligently;
- avoid constant praise of the player;
- allow embarrassment, frustration, pride and repair;
- preserve local flavor without caricaturing Romanian people or Brăila;
- use institutional/business language only when it helps understanding.

Avoid:

- superhero language;
- destiny/chosen-one framing;
- villain monologues;
- fake “corporate evil” shorthand;
- endless tutorials disguised as conversations;
- melodrama unsupported by gameplay;
- copyrighted franchise imitation;
- blockchain/NFT plot framing.

---

# 15. Narrative Quality Gate

Every major narrative proposal should answer:

1. Which real person, organization, place or system does the player care about here?
2. What does the player want?
3. What prevents an easy solution?
4. Which authoritative gameplay systems create the constraint?
5. What meaningful choice can the player make?
6. What can change visibly or persistently afterward?
7. Can the player recover if they fail?
8. Does this beat still work for a non-founder career path?
9. Can another World Instance produce a different later history?
10. Does the beat strengthen **Your work leaves a mark**?

If the only answer is “the player gets money/XP for a delivery,” the narrative beat is insufficient.

# Canonical Narrative Rule

**DROPi Tycoon tells the story of an ordinary world-local person who begins poor and on foot in Brăila, earns trust through useful work, develops real capability, forms lasting relationships with people and places, chooses a career and organizational path, accepts responsibility for increasingly large economic/logistics systems, expands through Romania and beyond only when that reach is earned, and eventually leaves a professional, organizational or world-scale legacy. The story is carried by authoritative simulation, recurring characters and persistent consequences rather than magical rewards or a mandatory CEO destiny.**
