This document defines the reusable creative advertising and cinematic language for DROPi Tycoon. It is a creative-production authority only. It does not promote a gameplay mechanic, build, asset, release state or legal state beyond the authority of the owning specialist.

The governing production rule is:

> **Make the viewer feel the world before explaining the world, but never make cinematic imagination look like shipped gameplay.**

The governing truth rule is:

> **Market the playable truth. Preserve future ambition as future ambition.**

At this baseline, #317 remains an open player-visible quality gate, #565 remains an open commercial provenance/legal gate, #568 remains an open production-AAB/release-attestation gate, and #643 remains an open global-functional-locality gate. Therefore this Bible creates concepts, treatments, capture requirements and edit structures; it does not declare a public launch trailer or production gameplay claim ready.

# 1. Emotional identity

DROPi Tycoon advertising should feel like **ordinary life becoming unexpectedly large**.

The emotional curve is:

`small human beginning -> useful work -> trust -> possibility -> capability -> responsibility -> scale -> living world`

The viewer should not first feel that they are looking at a menu of features. The viewer should first feel:

- I begin with very little.
- One ordinary task matters to another person.
- The city is not scenery; it reacts, remembers and creates pressure.
- Progress is earned rather than granted.
- A job can become a skill, a relationship, an asset, a team or a company.
- My path does not have to be the same as another player's path.
- The street in front of me belongs to something much larger.

## 1.1 Emotional tone

Primary tone:

- grounded;
- hopeful;
- ambitious;
- human;
- observant;
- quietly cinematic rather than bombastic;
- curious about systems without becoming a spreadsheet advertisement.

Avoid:

- fake luxury fantasy;
- instant-riches language;
- generic corporate-tech advertising;
- exaggerated claims of domination or infinite wealth;
- presenting the player as a predestined hero;
- combat-trailer grammar that does not fit the game;
- feature-wall editing with no emotional progression.

## 1.2 Core emotional line

The central reusable creative idea is:

> **One small job can become the beginning of something much larger.**

Supporting canonical narrative ideas include:

- `Your work leaves a mark.`
- a person before a market;
- a relationship before a network;
- a route before a company;
- a street before a world.

# 2. Reusable campaign themes

## Theme A — ONE SMALL THING

**Promise:** a tiny action can matter.

Creative grammar:
- one package;
- one route;
- one person waiting;
- one completed responsibility;
- one visible next possibility.

Best for: 6–15 second ads, first-install creative, creator intros.

Public use rule: the delivery and consequence shown as gameplay must be captured from the exact validated build. Cinematic metaphor may dramatize scale, but must not imply an unavailable systemic consequence.

## Theme B — ZERO TO SOMETHING

**Promise:** progression begins with scarcity, work and earned trust.

Creative grammar:
- walking;
- basic smartphone;
- backpack/carrying capability;
- first shift;
- first legitimate settlement;
- later capability horizon.

Best for: 15–30 second ads, progression montages, before/after edits.

Important: never imply the player is guaranteed to become a founder, millionaire or CEO.

## Theme C — THE CITY IS ALIVE

**Promise:** the player works inside a place with people, movement and local pressures.

Creative grammar:
- crossings;
- traffic;
- pedestrians;
- storefronts;
- depot/workplace activity;
- household and merchant endpoints;
- readable locality identity.

Best for: city spotlights, YouTube trailers, scenic B-roll.

Public use rule: every visible living-city behavior must exist in the captured candidate. Staged cinematic crowds are `CINEMATIC / NON-GAMEPLAY`, not gameplay proof.

## Theme D — YOUR LIFE, YOUR ROUTE

**Promise:** work creates choices, but systems determine what is legitimately available.

Creative grammar:
- forked visual routes;
- different jobs/capabilities/opportunities;
- relationship and career horizons;
- choice without promising unsupported branching.

Best for: 15–30 second social, narrative teasers.

## Theme E — BUILD SOMETHING BIGGER

**Promise:** responsibility can scale from personal work to organizational responsibility.

Creative grammar:
- one worker;
- team coordination;
- fleet/asset/company imagery only when player-facing evidence exists;
- wider operational view.

Best for: 30–60 second progression/tycoon creative.

Important: Personal Money and Company Money must never be visually or verbally collapsed into one wallet.

## Theme F — HUMAN STORIES

**Promise:** routes connect recurring people, not only markers.

Creative grammar:
- dispatcher/mentor;
- coworker/peer;
- merchant/customer;
- household customer;
- recurring recognition;
- authored consequence.

Best for: character teasers, story trailers, update films.

Brăila names and identities remain Brăila-specific. For global creative, advertise narrative functions only when corresponding locality-specific playable content exists.

## Theme G — THE WORLD OPENS

**Promise:** the player's mental horizon expands beyond the first street.

Creative grammar:
- street -> area -> city -> wider strategic view;
- map-scale transitions;
- routes becoming networks;
- lights/lines connecting beyond the immediate frame.

Best for: flagship cinematic material and later global campaigns.

Current restriction: do not claim that visible world catalog coverage equals globally playable cities. Multi-locality gameplay remains a future public claim until #643 evidence exists.

# 4. Flagship treatment — “From One Delivery to a Living World”

Target duration: **75 seconds**, adaptable to 60–90 seconds.

## 4.1 Objective

Introduce DROPi Tycoon as a human-scale economic life and logistics game in which small useful actions can grow into larger responsibility and possibility.

The film should make the viewer emotionally enter the world **before** normal gameplay becomes the primary evidence surface.

## 4.2 Creative device — “The Package Becomes the World”

The film begins in darkness with a small parcel and ordinary ambient sound. Its tape line becomes a road. The road becomes a district route. The route becomes a network. The network becomes a larger world horizon.

This transformation is symbolic.

Classification: `CINEMATIC / NON-GAMEPLAY`.

It must not be edited to look like direct control footage.

# 7. Truth classification system

Every storyboard frame, raw capture, motion-graphics element and final edit marker must use one of these classes.

## `AUTHENTIC GAMEPLAY`

Requirements:
- captured from an exact identified game build;
- mechanic exists in that build;
- no fake UI, numbers, objectives or state;
- no edit changes the factual result of the gameplay;
- capture retains SHA/build/device metadata;
- public use requires applicable DT-01/DT-14/DT-13/DT-15 quality and release gates.

## `CINEMATIC / NON-GAMEPLAY`

Allowed:
- symbolic transitions;
- pre-rendered compositions;
- dramatic camera concepts;
- governed character/world art;
- title cards;
- atmospheric narrative montage.

Rules:
- do not disguise it as direct control footage;
- do not add fake gameplay UI;
- do not show a roadmap feature in a way that reasonably communicates “this is available now.”

## `FUTURE CREATIVE — NOT PUBLIC CLAIM READY`

Use for powerful creative that depends on missing release evidence, including:
- multi-locality playable journeys;
- mature visible company operation if current presentation is not release-evidenced;
- full causal production/supply/demand loops;
- persistent authored consequences not yet proven in candidate gameplay;
- global relocation/continuity;
- future transport/infrastructure modes;
- any feature only present in design/canon/source foundations without release-visible proof.

# 10. Music, ambience, SFX and voice-over direction

## 10.1 Music

Creative shape:
- begin almost dry;
- introduce one warm pulse after the first human action;
- build with restrained rhythmic layers as responsibility grows;
- widen harmonically at city/world scale;
- return to a human-scale final note for `Your work leaves a mark.`

Suggested musical qualities:
- warm low percussion;
- organic clicks/taps suggesting work without sounding industrially harsh;
- subtle synth bed for routes/technology;
- restrained piano/plucked motif for human story;
- no generic “epic trailer” brass wall.

No track is assumed licensed. Any production music must be DT-13 cleared with provider/license/term evidence or be governed project-owned original audio with documented rights.

## 10.2 Ambience

Useful layers when legitimate:
- footsteps;
- distant traffic;
- bicycle/vehicle movement;
- river/wind ambience where location-appropriate;
- depot/warehouse room tone;
- storefront/city pedestrian texture;
- subtle phone interaction sounds.

Never add ambience that falsely implies a gameplay system exists in the captured scene.

## 10.3 SFX

The sound design should connect small actions into momentum:

`tape -> phone cue -> pickup -> footsteps/transport -> arrival -> settlement/consequence -> route expansion`

A cinematic transition SFX may be non-diegetic, but actual gameplay UI sounds shown as part of the game should come from the captured/game-approved sound set where possible.

## 10.4 Voice-over

Voice style:
- intimate;
- observant;
- calm confidence;
- not announcer-heavy;
- short sentences;
- allow visual pauses.

Flagship VO draft:

> It starts small. One shift. One job someone is waiting for. Then it becomes trust. A skill. A choice. Work can become capability. Capability can become responsibility. Every route can become a relationship. Every relationship can become a network. Your work leaves a mark.

This is creative copy, not a gameplay-status claim. Final public wording must be reconciled with release evidence.

Voice identity, performer rights, synthetic-voice terms and localization rights require DT-13 clearance.

# 11. Gameplay capture checklist

Before capture:

- [ ] record exact Git SHA;
- [ ] record exact Android/build identity;
- [ ] confirm candidate is the intended truth source;
- [ ] identify claim IDs for the planned shot;
- [ ] confirm no branch-only/unmerged visual is being presented as release gameplay;
- [ ] confirm no debug/dev overlay dominates the shot;
- [ ] confirm required third-party attribution remains intact;
- [ ] confirm state used in the capture is legitimate gameplay, not manually edited save/state;
- [ ] confirm character/story beat is triggered by legitimate authoritative state;
- [ ] confirm no confidential account/device information is visible.

During capture:

- [ ] capture raw native-quality landscape footage;
- [ ] keep 5–10 seconds of handles before/after each key action;
- [ ] capture one continuous end-to-end delivery loop where possible;
- [ ] capture clean city traversal at multiple truthful camera scales;
- [ ] capture phone/order state with readable live values;
- [ ] capture delivery completion and actual resulting feedback;
- [ ] capture story presentation only when it is tied to real runtime events;
- [ ] avoid repeated artificial loops that suggest more traffic/citizens than exist;
- [ ] record device/resolution/orientation and capture method.

After capture:

- [ ] preserve original raw files;
- [ ] hash/name/source-map the footage;
- [ ] record crop/stabilization/speed changes;
- [ ] never alter money/reward/timer/objective values in post;
- [ ] map every final gameplay shot to exact evidence/build provenance;
- [ ] invalidate/re-capture footage if the advertised behavior changes before release.

# 12. Editing checklist

Truth:
- [ ] every shot has a truth class;
- [ ] cinematic footage cannot be mistaken for authentic gameplay;
- [ ] future creative is excluded from a current-feature public cut;
- [ ] supers do not overclaim the shot beneath them;
- [ ] no fake ratings/reviews/player counts/awards/downloads;
- [ ] no release/platform claim without owning authority.

Story:
- [ ] opening hook is understandable without sound;
- [ ] viewer sees a human need before system scale;
- [ ] progression is earned rather than magical;
- [ ] the edit returns to a human consequence after large-scale imagery;
- [ ] CTA follows the actual destination/status.

Visual:
- [ ] maintain canonical stylized 3D pre-rendered identity where cinematic material is used;
- [ ] avoid incompatible stock visual styles;
- [ ] preserve readable gameplay framing;
- [ ] do not fake portrait-mode gameplay;
- [ ] avoid excessive zoom cuts that make city scale unreadable.

Audio:
- [ ] music/SFX/VO provenance recorded;
- [ ] dialogue remains understandable on phone speakers;
- [ ] silent/subtitled variant works without audio;
- [ ] no copyrighted/unlicensed placeholder audio survives export.

Delivery:
- [ ] 16:9 master;
- [ ] 9:16 social derivative;
- [ ] 1:1 derivative where useful;
- [ ] text-safe zones checked;
- [ ] subtitles burned or sidecar prepared as required;
- [ ] version name includes creative ID / date / language / truth-state.
