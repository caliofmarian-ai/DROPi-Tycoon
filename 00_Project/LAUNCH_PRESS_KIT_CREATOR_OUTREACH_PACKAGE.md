# 5. Developer / project story

## 5.1 Canonical short version

> DROPi Tycoon is an independent AI-assisted game project led through a detailed canonical design and verification workflow. The project begins from Brăila, Romania, using logistics as the bridge between human-scale work and a much larger long-term business-tycoon vision. The design goal is to make the player feel present in a city rather than operating a detached administration dashboard, while making growth feel earned through work, capability, relationships and increasingly complex economic responsibility.

## 5.2 Longer press-context version

> DROPi Tycoon began from a simple question: what if a logistics tycoon did not start with an abstract company screen, but with one visible person trying to become useful in a city?
>
> The project uses Brăila as its opening context and builds outward from local work toward a broader business and logistics simulation. Its canonical vision combines human-scale presence, business progression, logistics systems and a living economic world, while deliberately keeping future systems such as global networks, multiplayer and drone simulation separated from what the current build can honestly claim.
>
> Development is AI-assisted and repository-driven. Features are specified, implemented and audited through versioned documents, issues, branches, pull requests, deterministic tests and Android acceptance gates. The same discipline applies to marketing: a feature is not promoted simply because it exists in a design document or domain model; it must be present and validated in the build being shown.

## 5.3 Story guardrails

The project story may discuss:

- Brăila as the opening inspiration/context;
- the human-scale-to-business progression philosophy;
- the game-first principle;
- the AI-assisted repository workflow;
- the ambition to build a deep logistics/economic game over time.

The project story must not imply:

- OpenAI sponsorship, endorsement or commercial partnership;
- that AI-generated work has bypassed human/owner review;
- that the real DROPi delivery application and DROPi Tycoon are the same product;
- guaranteed real-world business, logistics or financial outcomes;
- that the long-term Vision document equals current release functionality.

## 14.2 Current earned-outreach model

- unpaid;
- no sponsorship fee;
- no affiliate commission;
- no required coverage;
- no required positive sentiment;
- no editorial control;
- possible access to a non-public test build once separately approved.

Because non-public access can itself be relevant context for an audience, the project should encourage plain-language transparency such as:

`Review/test access was provided by the developer; no payment and no approval of this coverage.`

Use the creator/platform/jurisdiction’s required disclosure mechanism in addition where applicable.

## 14.3 EU guidance

European Commission Influencer Legal Hub guidance explains that commercial advertising relationships, including content created in exchange for products or services and affiliate marketing, require disclosure under applicable EU consumer law.

Reference:

https://commission.europa.eu/topics/consumers/consumer-rights-and-complaints/influencer-legal-hub_en

The Commission’s influencer enforcement material also emphasizes that commercial disclosure should be clear and visible rather than hidden behind extra clicks or vague language.

## 14.4 United States guidance

FTC guidance says influencers should disclose material connections with brands and that financial relationships are not limited to money; free or discounted products and other perks can require disclosure.

Reference:

https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers

## 14.5 United Kingdom guidance

For UK-facing outreach, ASA/CAP guidance distinguishes payment/benefit and editorial control and emphasizes obvious identification of advertising where the applicable tests are met.

Reference:

https://www.asa.org.uk/news/like-follow-and-subscribe-to-our-influencer-marketing-advice.html

# 17. Initial unpaid micro-creator targeting criteria

## 17.1 Targeting principle

**Audience fit beats follower count.**

The first earned cohort should teach the project whether its human-scale logistics/tycoon premise resonates with the right players.

## 17.2 Priority lanes

Priority 1:

- tycoon / management simulation creators;
- logistics / transport / supply-chain simulation creators;
- indie simulation discovery creators.

Priority 2:

- Android/mobile creators who regularly cover substantive management/simulation games;
- Romanian-language gaming creators with an authentic reason to care about a Brăila/Romania-rooted project;
- English-language European indie creators with a simulation audience.

Priority 3:

- broader strategy creators whose audience demonstrates real management/simulation interest;
- Shorts/Reels/TikTok creators capable of explaining systems rather than only showing spectacle.

Do not initially prioritize generic high-follower entertainment accounts with no simulation fit.

## 17.3 Internal scoring rubric

Score 0–5 for each dimension, then apply the weight.

| Dimension | Weight | What good looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| Tycoon/simulation/logistics audience fit | 30 | Repeated relevant coverage and audience response |
| Format fit | 15 | Can show/explain Android landscape gameplay clearly |
| Engagement quality | 15 | Real comments/discussion, not only raw follower count |
| Indie discovery history | 15 | Willingness to cover small/pre-release games fairly |
| Language/market fit | 10 | English or Romanian first; expansion evidence-driven |
| Disclosure/editorial professionalism | 10 | Clear labeling, independent tone, no pay-for-praise behavior |
| Contactability | 5 | Public professional contact route and reasonable response history |

Maximum score: 100.

Initial research threshold recommendation:

`>= 70` for the first contact shortlist.

This is an internal prioritization rule, not an industry definition of a micro-creator.

## 17.4 First cohort size

Research:

- 20–30 high-fit channels/accounts.

When outreach is authorized:

- contact an initial 10–20 personalized candidates;
- do not mass-email hundreds of creators;
- learn from response rate, build friction and coverage quality before expanding.

## 17.5 Disqualifiers / hold criteria

Put a creator on `HOLD` or exclude from the first wave when:

- coverage is primarily unrelated to games/simulation;
- the creator requires payment before any factual conversation and no paid budget is authorized;
- the creator requires the developer to purchase positive sentiment/rating;
- engagement appears materially artificial or bot-driven;
- the creator routinely hides commercial relationships;
- the audience is predominantly children while the project’s target-audience/Play minor decisions remain unresolved;
- the proposed content would require unsupported global/multiplayer/drone/story claims;
- there is no legitimate public business contact route.
