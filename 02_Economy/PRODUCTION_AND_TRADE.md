# Production and Trade Economy

## Purpose

This document defines how production, supply chains, inventories, contracts, logistics and regional price differences create an active economy in DROPi Tycoon.

It specializes `02_Economy/ECONOMY.md`, `02_Economy/MARKET.md`, `00_Project/BUSINESS_DESIGN.md` and `00_Project/LOGISTICS_DESIGN.md`.

Goods are not unlimited abstract income. They come from productive capacity, inputs, people, infrastructure and time.

---

# 1. Productive Nodes

Productive facilities may exist inside localities or independently in external geography.

Canonical families may include:

- farms/agriculture and livestock;
- forestry;
- mining/quarrying;
- steel/metallurgy;
- paper/pulp;
- chemical and fertilizer production;
- refining/fuels;
- power/energy;
- construction materials;
- machinery/components;
- food processing;
- consumer goods;
- medical/pharma and technology when activated;
- warehousing/distribution;
- industrial ports and freight terminals.

A productive node has location, operating state, ownership/control, capacity, required inputs, outputs, inventory, workforce/specialist requirements and transport access.

---

# 2. Production Chains

Production recipes transform inputs into outputs over time.

Representative game abstractions include:

**Industrial chemicals -> fertilizer -> farms -> crops -> food processor -> local marketplace/customer.**

**Raw materials + energy -> steel -> machinery/construction materials -> infrastructure and city growth.**

**Timber -> pulp/paper -> packaging -> merchants/factories -> parcel/logistics demand.**

Recipes should be recognizable enough to create strategic dependency without becoming industrial-engineering simulations.

---

# 3. Demand, Supply and Inventory

Demand can originate from:

- consumers;
- marketplaces;
- productive facilities requiring inputs;
- construction/infrastructure;
- public/service needs;
- contracts;
- population growth;
- tourism/events.

Supply depends on actual production, inventory, imports and transport access.

Price pressure may respond to combinations of:

- local inventory;
- demand intensity;
- production capacity;
- distance and transport cost;
- congestion;
- season/weather;
- shortages/surpluses;
- contract commitments;
- events;
- future tariff/currency abstractions.

Prices may legitimately differ between localities, regions and countries, creating logistics/trade opportunities.

---

# 4. Local Marketplaces and Trade

Each representative locality can have a local marketplace supplied by local merchants/producers, nearby external nodes, regional warehouses, national suppliers and imports.

A mature player company may choose where to send owned/contracted output by comparing sale price, demand, logistics cost, time, capacity, risk, storage and contractual obligations.

The highest nominal price is not automatically the most profitable destination.

---

# 5. Delivery Company Progression With Producers

## Stage 1 — Occasional Jobs
A farm, factory, steelworks, mill or plant may publish individual cargo jobs caused by inventory, input shortage or buyer demand.

## Stage 2 — Recurring Contracts
A logistics company can earn recurring agreements based on capacity, price, reliability, reputation, specialist capability, vehicle/infrastructure suitability and geographic coverage.

## Stage 3 — Strategic Logistics Partnership
A mature carrier may operate dedicated routes, warehouses, multimodal legs or supporting infrastructure for a producer.

## Stage 4 — Productive Asset Acquisition
Where finance, governance, workforce, specialist and world rules allow it, a mature company may acquire eligible farms, factories, plants, warehouses, terminals or industrial complexes.

## Stage 5 — Integrated Producer + Logistics Operator
The company can produce, store, route and sell goods while remaining responsible for all modeled inputs, people, energy, maintenance, capacity and demand.

Ownership never creates free output or guaranteed profit.

---

# 6. Contracts

A producer/logistics contract may define:

- origin and destination;
- cargo/product group;
- volume;
- frequency;
- service/deadline window;
- revenue/payment basis;
- penalties/reputation consequences;
- reserved capacity;
- duration;
- renewal/termination conditions.

Contract cargo must remain connected to real inventory and logistics capacity when those systems are active.

---

# 7. Economic Dependencies and Development

Products can unlock or accelerate other productive activity.

Example:

1. a chemical producer supplies required chemicals;
2. a fertilizer plant produces fertilizer;
3. fertilizer expands agricultural output where land, season, workers and infrastructure permit;
4. farms create food/raw-goods supply;
5. processors and marketplaces consume/distribute those goods;
6. additional commerce creates jobs, logistics demand and population support;
7. growth creates new needs for housing, construction, energy, specialists and transport.

No single commodity should automatically create prosperity. Growth requires complementary systems.

---

# 8. Urban and Rural Profiles

Urban areas typically concentrate consumer demand, services, construction, industry and high logistics volume.

Rural areas may specialize in agriculture, livestock, forestry, extraction, processing, tourism or specialist local industry while importing machinery, chemicals, consumer products, energy or services.

This distinction should create different cargo flows and company opportunities.

---

# 9. Ownership and Fair Access

Productive assets may be controlled by NPC/simulated companies, player companies, public/world institutions or future governed concession/lease structures.

Critical essential supply categories require alternative suppliers, public baseline capacity or recovery mechanisms so one owner cannot permanently starve an entire World Instance.

---

# 10. Simulation Frequency

Production/trade does not run per frame.

Typical responsibility split:

- active cargo movement — route/local frequency;
- production/inventory — economic tick;
- contracts/pricing — market cycle;
- industrial investment — strategic cycle;
- structural economic change — seasonal/yearly cycle.

Exact timing remains configuration/balance data.

---

# 11. Real-World Inspiration Boundary

Broad regional specializations may be inspired by real geography/economy, but runtime values are fictional simulation state. The game does not claim live official output, prices, ownership or forecasts.

New baseline datasets may change future World Instances but must not silently rewrite an existing world's historical economy.

---

# Canonical Rule

**World demand creates business opportunity; productive capacity creates supply; inventories and logistics connect them. A delivery company can evolve from occasional carrier to contracted network and finally an integrated producer-logistics corporation without bypassing real modeled costs, dependencies and capacity.**
