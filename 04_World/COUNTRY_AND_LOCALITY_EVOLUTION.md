# Country and Locality Evolution

Status: Canonical specialization
Last Updated: 2026-09-08

## Purpose

This document defines long-term development and decline for countries, cities, rural localities and undeveloped territory.

## Growth inputs

Growth may require combinations of:

- food;
- energy;
- construction materials;
- employment;
- housing;
- transport/logistics access;
- specialist availability;
- industrial output;
- services;
- trade access;
- investment;
- infrastructure.

No single metric creates growth automatically.

## Visible city evolution

As a locality grows, the detailed scene and strategic map may reflect:

- more housing;
- denser architecture;
- more diverse building families;
- new businesses;
- new industry;
- expanded roads/rail/utilities;
- more people and workers;
- new specialist facilities;
- higher marketplace activity.

## Rural evolution

Rural areas can develop through:

- agriculture;
- livestock;
- forestry;
- extraction;
- food processing;
- tourism;
- specialist industry;
- improved transport links.

Rural development must preserve rural identity rather than becoming a visually smaller city.

## New settlements

Advanced world simulation may create a new town/city in eligible undeveloped territory only when a plausible foundation exists, such as resources, jobs, transport, investment, infrastructure and population inflow.

## Migration

Population may move according to fictional world conditions including jobs, wages, housing, services, economic opportunity, infrastructure, environment and major events.

Specialist migration may unlock or constrain industries and infrastructure.

## Decline

Long-term negative pressure can produce:

- unemployment;
- out-migration;
- abandoned housing/industry;
- reduced services;
- lower production;
- shrinking marketplace demand;
- deteriorating infrastructure;
- desertification or environmental decline where world conditions support it.

Normal decline must preserve recovery paths.

## Country development

Country-level strength may emerge from a basket of:

- productivity;
- trade;
- infrastructure;
- food/energy security;
- workforce and specialists;
- innovation;
- logistics capacity;
- population trend;
- tourism/services;
- macro events.

Future national-currency mechanics may reflect these fundamentals through a fictional exchange system without claiming to reproduce live real-world rates.

## State changes

Advanced world instances may allow a country's political state to merge, split, become inactive or later re-emerge after macro events. Geography and historical identifiers remain preserved.

These are fictional game histories, not predictions about real countries.

## Canonical rule

**Countries and localities are outcomes of the living economy over time. Their population, architecture, industry, infrastructure and strategic importance can grow or decline according to connected world systems.**
