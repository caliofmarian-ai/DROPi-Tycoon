# R9 — Comparative Design Translation Matrix

Status: **RESEARCH WORKING PAPER — NON-CANONICAL**
Parent: #423

## Purpose

Translate proven engagement principles from reference games and player-motivation research into original DROPi Tycoon design rules without copying protected expression, branded content, maps, characters, art, text or exact proprietary mechanics.

The question is not `what feature can we copy?` It is:

> What player need does the reference game satisfy, and how can DROPi satisfy the same need through its own logistics/economic identity?

# Translation principles

## 1. Borrow the motivation, not the expression

A reference is useful only when we can name the human motivation behind it.

Examples:

- `shared road` -> **my effort helped strangers**;
- `custom truck` -> **this productive tool is mine**;
- `restored community building` -> **the world remembers my work**;
- `factory automation` -> **I transformed my own production method**;
- `new city district` -> **my strategic choice changed physical space**.

DROPi must express those motivations through its own economy, logistics, professions, infrastructure and society.

## 2. One mechanic should satisfy multiple motivations where possible

Example: buying the first bicycle can simultaneously provide:

- competence: higher route efficiency;
- ownership: a visible personal asset;
- autonomy: different route/job choices;
- progression: new work eligibility;
- economic strategy: maintenance versus walking-energy trade-off;
- visual satisfaction: bicycle exists physically in the world.

Such mechanics are more valuable than isolated reward systems.

## 3. Prefer systemic variety over mission-text variety

Good variety comes from changing conditions:

- cargo requirements;
- traffic;
- weather;
- time windows;
- customer/facility types;
- local prices and shortages;
- construction;
- industry state;
- route/infrastructure changes;
- skill/equipment choices.

Changing only mission wording does not create meaningful replayability.

## 4. Progress must transform verbs

A progression unlock is strongest when it changes what the player can *do*.

Examples:

- walk -> cycle -> drive -> operate multimodal networks;
- carry envelopes -> cold-chain -> industrial/special cargo;
- take shifts -> plan routes -> dispatch others -> negotiate contracts;
- deliver to shops -> supply factories -> operate production -> control supply chains;
- use infrastructure -> finance/build/operate infrastructure.

`+5% reward` may support balance but should not be the core fantasy of advancement.

## 5. Community meaning must not erase solo agency

A solo player must always have meaningful work, development and discovery through NPC/simulation continuity.

Community adds:

- human employers/employees;
- contracts;
- projects;
- mentorship;
- competition;
- cooperation;
- historical recognition.

It must not become a gate that says `find four online players or stop progressing`.

## 6. The world is the reward surface

Wherever practical, reward important actions through persistent change before adding abstract reward UI.

Examples:

- construction advances;
- shelves refill;
- worker appears;
- vehicle appears in garage;
- factory restarts;
- road opens;
- district becomes busier/cleaner;
- new business opens;
- migration changes population activity.

Money, reputation and qualifications remain important, but the world should confirm them physically.

---

# Comparative rejection rules

Do not import a reference mechanic when it would:

- contradict the authoritative stock/flow economy;
- create magical resources or money;
- make logistics decorative;
- force synchronous multiplayer for basic progression;
- create pay-to-win or FOMO;
- replace player choice with one optimal grind;
- overwhelm Android/mobile interaction;
- require a simulation detail level that cannot scale globally;
- undermine DROPi's approved visual/world identity.

---

## Research conclusion

DROPi should combine the *motivational strengths* of life simulation, transport simulation, production/automation, city building, exploration and persistent multiplayer organizations, but all of them must resolve into one original promise:

> **I live in this economy, I learn to operate more of it, and the world visibly changes because of the work I chose to do.**
