# R3 — Professions, Qualifications and Labor Economy Working Model

Status: **RESEARCH WORKING PAPER — NON-CANONICAL**
Parent: #423
Track: #427

## Purpose

Define how people become economically useful specialists, how companies hire and pay them, how labor shortages affect development, and how one human player can build a career without being forced to own a company.

---

# 1. Labor is productive capacity

A job is not a decorative title.

A worker contributes one or more capabilities such as:

- manual labor;
- vehicle operation;
- handling/cargo eligibility;
- maintenance;
- dispatch/planning;
- production operation;
- technical supervision;
- management/authority;
- research/training;
- finance/commercial capability.

A company that lacks required labor/specialists cannot safely/productively operate the corresponding activity at full capability.

---

# 2. Profession portfolio, not class lock

A person can accumulate multiple qualifications and profession masteries over time.

Examples:

- courier + bicycle operator;
- van driver + warehouse operator;
- mechanic + fleet supervisor;
- drone operator + drone technician;
- maritime operator + port cargo specialist;
- chemical plant operator + hazardous-cargo handler;
- finance specialist + company executive.

A profession is a capability family; employment is a relationship; company membership is a relationship; executive authority is a permission state. These must remain separate.

---

# 3. Qualification structure

A qualification can require a combination of:

```text
prerequisite knowledge
+ theory/study
+ practical training
+ supervised work
+ equipment/facility access
+ assessment/exam
+ fee/cost
+ authorization/license abstraction
= qualification
```

Higher-risk/high-impact fields should require stronger gates than ordinary entry work.

---

# 4. Training channels

Training can come from multiple economic institutions:

- self-study / phone theory modules;
- public/basic training center;
- private school/training provider;
- employer-sponsored course;
- apprenticeship;
- company department/workshop;
- university/technical institute abstraction;
- simulator;
- supervised practical workplace.

Training itself creates demand for instructors, facilities, equipment and time.

---

# 5. Entry profession ladder

The owner-defined starting path:

```text
Pedestrian leaflet/letter courier
-> small-parcel pedestrian courier
-> bicycle/light mobility courier
-> powered light-vehicle courier
-> driver / van courier
```

From there the person can branch rather than follow one mandatory ladder:

```text
                    COURIER / DRIVER
                         |
      +------------------+------------------+
      |                  |                  |
      v                  v                  v
 OPERATIONS          TECHNICAL          BUSINESS
 dispatch/warehouse  maintenance        entrepreneurship
      |                  |                  |
      v                  v                  v
 multimodal ops      specialist tech     management
```

# 7. Job opening model

A company creates a job because it has unmet labor capability.

```text
planned/actual demand
-> required productive capacity
-> current workforce capacity checked
-> shortage detected
-> job opening / contract posted
-> eligible humans/NPCs apply/are matched
-> employment agreement settles
```

No company should hire purely to satisfy a level requirement without a useful role.

---

# 8. Employment contract model

A job relationship may include:

- employer;
- worker;
- role/capability;
- location/facility;
- schedule/availability model;
- wage/salary terms;
- performance/quality expectations;
- required qualifications;
- equipment responsibility;
- duration/probation;
- termination rules;
- promotion/training rights where relevant.

Starting work uses day/shift wage settlement.

---

# 9. Wage formation

Wages should not be one global static table forever.

Candidate wage influences:

- local cost of living;
- profession scarcity;
- required qualification;
- work difficulty/energy demand;
- schedule;
- responsibility/risk;
- employer profitability/capacity;
- competition for labor;
- local unemployment;
- negotiated/posted terms.

A company that pays too little may fail to attract/retain specialists.

A company that overpays may become unprofitable.

---

# 10. Human vs NPC labor

Both use one economic role model.

NPC labor is necessary for continuity and low population.

Human labor can be more valuable through:

- better decisions;
- adaptive routing;
- negotiation;
- rare specialization;
- leadership;
- entrepreneurship;
- cooperative teamwork;
- handling unusual events;
- high-value strategic tasks.

NPCs can provide baseline predictable output, but they still require wages/resources and should have local availability/scarcity.

Do not make NPC labor free infinite automation.

---

# 11. Labor pool and population

A city/region has finite available labor.

Labor availability depends on:

- population;
- age/workforce abstraction;
- education/qualification distribution;
- unemployment;
- commuting/transport;
- wages;
- housing;
- migration;
- quality/cost of living;
- competing employers.

An industrial boom can therefore create labor shortages, wage increases, housing demand and migration.

---

# 12. Specialist scarcity

Specialists should unlock development through real capacity.

Example:

```text
city attracts chemical plant
-> plant needs chemical-process operators + maintenance + logistics
-> insufficient specialists
-> wages rise / training demand rises
-> companies sponsor education / workers migrate
-> specialist pool grows
-> plant reaches higher output
```

Specialist scarcity must create opportunity, not permanent deadlock. Recovery paths include training, migration, NPC recruitment, automation and lower-capability operation where appropriate.

---

# 13. Promotion

Promotion should change authority, compensation or responsibility.

Possible promotion factors:

- work history/reliability;
- profession mastery;
- additional qualifications;
- leadership capability;
- employer need;
- available vacancy;
- performance;
- company growth.

Promotion is not guaranteed merely because XP reached a number.

---

# 14. Career switching

A person can change field.

Existing qualifications remain in the portfolio unless a specific authorization expires/revokes.

Switching may require:

- new training;
- lower starting seniority in the new field;
- new equipment;
- geographic relocation;
- temporary income reduction.

This makes retraining a strategic personal decision.

---

# 15. Employer-sponsored development

Companies can invest in employees.

Examples:

- pay training fee;
- provide equipment;
- provide supervised hours;
- sponsor qualification;
- offer promotion pathway.

Possible reciprocal terms need later research:

- minimum employment period;
- repayment only under fair bounded rules;
- retention bonus;
- no abusive permanent lock-in.

This creates competition for talent and gives players a way to progress without personally funding every qualification.

---

# 16. Work Capacity and labor productivity

R1 finite Work Capacity connects to R3.

Manual professions consume more personal capacity than mechanized/automated work.

Productivity can depend on:

- energy/rest state;
- equipment;
- skill/mastery;
- route/work organization;
- facility quality;
- automation;
- safety/maintenance;
- workload.

Do not equate "more hours online" with unlimited productivity.

---

# 17. Unemployment and recovery

A person without a job remains an economic actor.

Possible actions:

- apply to another employer;
- accept entry work;
- take temporary/gig contracts;
- study/retrain;
- move to a labor-short region;
- sell services independently when qualified;
- form a company when eligible.

Unemployment can reduce income and create financial pressure, but cannot permanently softlock identity.

---

# 18. Labor migration

Wage/job differences create migration incentives.

Workers may move because of:

- higher wages;
- available jobs;
- housing availability;
- lower living costs;
- specialist demand;
- quality/services;
- events/decline/conflict.

Transport infrastructure affects whether migration/commuting is practical.

---

# 19. Education economy

Schools/training institutions consume:

- instructors;
- facilities;
- equipment;
- money/funding;
- time;
- utilities/materials where relevant.

They produce:

- qualifications;
- specialist labor supply;
- research capability.

Education capacity therefore influences city/country economic development.

---

# 20. High-impact authorization

Some professions should require explicit authorization abstraction beyond simple training.

Candidates:

- commercial driving/heavy freight;
- hazardous cargo;
- rail operations;
- vessel/aircraft operation;
- drone operation;
- chemical/industrial safety;
- selected infrastructure control.

Authorization can be suspended/revoked only through clear gameplay rules and must have requalification/recovery paths.

---

# 21. Inactivity

A human who is offline does not perform unlimited manual labor.

Employment may remain as a relationship, but actual output depends on work/availability rules.

Long inactivity may lead to:

- missed shifts;
- replacement;
- termination;
- loss of operational appointment;
- not necessarily loss of earned qualification/history.

Companies need delegation/NPC replacement so one human absence cannot freeze essential operations.

---

# 22. Late-game career value

A player who never owns a company must still have late-game goals:

- rare/master specialist status;
- high compensation;
- international assignments;
- instructor/mentor status;
- strategic infrastructure operation;
- executive appointment;
- high-value contract work;
- reputation/legacy;
- contribution to major national/global projects.

Ownership is one path, not the definition of success.

---

# 23. Major unresolved R3 decisions

1. Exact job/salary cadence types by profession.
2. Whether starting hero has guaranteed employment or must apply after tutorial.
3. How employer-sponsored training obligations work.
4. Qualification renewal/expiry for high-impact professions.
5. NPC wage setting and availability generation.
6. Human productivity advantage vs NPC baseline.
7. Collective bargaining/unions — likely unnecessary unless later gameplay value is proven.
8. Maximum simultaneous employment relationships.
9. Part-time/multiple employers compatibility with internal company membership.
10. How remote/asynchronous professions operate.

---

## Research rule

**A profession is economically meaningful capability created by education, practice and authorization. Labor demand comes from real productive work; wages connect company costs to personal income; specialist scarcity changes investment, migration and city development; a human can build a full late-game career without becoming an owner.**
